# CYPURRPUNK roadmap

Written 9 Nov 2026. This is a living plan: we tick things off, and we rewrite it when playtests
teach us something. It is built around your answers:

- **Goal:** a free game that grows a real player community.
- **Time:** 5 to 15 hours a week.
- **Art:** no 3D modelling experience yet, willing to spend a little on tools.
- **Players today:** a few friends.

## The short version

1. **The game has plenty of systems and not enough reasons to care.** The next months should add
   almost no new weapons, enemies or rules. They should add identity, looks and a reason to play again.
2. **It is for friend groups who want to laugh together,** the people who play Lethal Company,
   R.E.P.O. and Peak. Build for 2 to 4 friends in co-op first. Gang-versus-gang becomes a side mode.
3. **The soul is "cats are the mess a clean-freak AI can't tidy up".** Right now you play a small
   gangster who happens to be a cat. The game gets its identity when you play a *cat*.
4. **Everything in the game is built from boxes and cylinders.** Replacing the cat and the VAC-COP
   with real low-poly models is the biggest single jump in how the game looks in a screenshot.
5. **Attention comes from Steam and short clips.** A free Steam page, Steam invites instead of
   router codes, and one funny 10-second clip a week.

## 1. Honest assessment

### What is strong
- **A complete loop.** Hub, raid, heat clock, payphone, extract, new district. Many hobby games
  never get this far.
- **A hook you can say in one breath.** Cats versus police robot vacuums.
- **Funny weapons.** Frozen Tuna, Red Dot, Cucumber Launcher. These are the game's best jokes.
- **One real set piece.** The Sewer Breach, with the Don spraying a tommy gun, is a story players
  will retell.
- **Easy to play together.** Split screen, invite codes, one-click downloads, a tutorial.

### What is thin
- **Nothing to work towards.** Cash buys the same 11 items. After two or three evenings a player
  owns everything. The stash and wallet have nothing meaningful to fill them for.
- **The city has nothing to find.** Every district is streets, buildings you can't enter, and fish
  on the pavement. A new seed moves things around but never surprises you.
- **You don't feel like a cat.** You run, jump, shoot and carry. None of that is cat behaviour.
- **A round has no ending.** No results screen, no score, no "who carried, who got robbed". The
  moment players would talk about the round is the moment the game says nothing.
- **It looks like a prototype in screenshots.** There is not one modelled 3D asset in the project:
  cat, cars, bots, NPCs and guns are all simple shapes. In motion with fog and the CRT effect it
  works. In a still image on a store page it reads as unfinished, not as PS1.
- **Two music tracks.** One for the city, one for the hub.
- **The rules are hard to explain.** Mainframe percentages, five different exit heat numbers, hacks,
  one trip per district, last raids. Each is fine alone. Together they need a guide.
- **PvP is built for 8 and played by 3.** Two gangs need at least 4 players to be fun. With a few
  friends, most of the gang work (two sewers, two cars, two exits, robbing, hacks) goes unused.

### What a new player feels
- **First 10 minutes:** charmed by the idea, slightly lost in the sewer, then a good first raid
  thanks to the tutorial.
- **First hour:** the best hour. First boss, first close escape, first Cucumber Launcher.
- **Tenth hour:** they have seen every enemy, bought every weapon and done all five quests.
  The fun runs out between hours 3 and 5.

## 2. Who this game is for

### Primary: the friend group (2 to 4 players on Discord or a couch)
- **They play:** Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., Peak, Content Warning, Overcooked.
- **They want:** to laugh at each other, short sessions, low stress, and moments worth clipping.
- **We lack:** an end-of-raid report, funny ways to fail, voice chat in the game, joining without
  router trouble, and something to do after being knocked out.

### Secondary: retro-look fans
- **They play:** low-poly and PS1-style indie games on itch.io and Steam Deck.
- **They want:** a look that is clearly a style choice, good music, a strong mood.
- **We lack:** real models, more music, and a few striking places in the city.

### Third: extraction players who find Tarkov too harsh
- **They play:** casual extraction games, and they like bringing loot home to a base.
- **They want:** risk, a stash that matters, a hideout that grows.
- **We lack:** anything to upgrade or unlock.

### Not for
- Competitive shooter players. The aiming and netcode are not built for that and should not be.
- Solo story players. There is no campaign and we are not making one.

## 3. Soul

### Three directions

| Direction | What it is | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Chaos versus Clean** | You are a cat. OVERCLEAN is a neat-freak smart home that took over a city. Making a mess is rebellion. | Unique, funny, every cat owner gets it at once. Gives new things to *do*. | Needs new cat actions and props to knock over. |
| **B. Mob family saga** | Godfather parody. The Don, the family, turf, respect. | Most of the writing already exists. | Seen before. It is flavour, and gives players nothing new to do. |
| **C. Serious rebellion** | Cyberpunk resistance against an AI. | Fits the mood and fog. | Fights the comedy weapons. Crowded genre. |

### Recommendation: A, with B as the costume
Keep the mob cats, the Don and Nonna. They are the cast. But the *game* is about being a cat in
a world that hates mess.

- **Pitch line:** "A co-op heist game where you are the mess a robot-vacuum city can't clean up."
- **OVERCLEAN gets a voice.** A calm, passive-aggressive robot voice over the city speakers:
  "A mess has been detected. Please remain where you are." "Shedding is a crime." It announces
  heat levels, bosses and the seal. This is cheap to make and gives the villain a personality.
- **Mess is a verb.** Knock bins, cones and shop displays over. Scratch posters. Push things off
  ledges. Mess distracts VAC-COPs (they must stop and clean it) but adds a little heat. Now
  the heat bar is a cleanliness alarm, and the bots are doing their job, which is funnier.
- **Cats do cat things.** A meow button that bots can hear. Always landing on your feet. Hiding in
  a cardboard box. Zoomies already exist: lean on that.
- **The retold story.** Every raid ends with a RAID REPORT: loot each cat brought home, mess made,
  bots smashed, who got vacuumed, plus one silly award each ("Most Shed", "Butterpaws").

| 4 | **Knocked-out cats spectate** team-mates and can ping | Small | Nobody sits staring at a sewer wall. |

| 10 | **In-game voice chat** that fades with distance | Large | The reason games like this spread. Do it after Steam. |

### What to cut, freeze or simplify
- **Freeze gang PvP.** Keep it as a mode called something like TURF WAR for when 4 or more
  play. Make co-op (one crew against OVERCLEAN) the default. No new PvP features this year.
- **Stop adding weapons and enemies.** 11 items and 4 bot types are enough until the rest catches up.
- **Simplify how the Mainframe is explained.** One sentence on screen: "More charge, earlier exit."
  Hide the percent tables. Hacking only appears in TURF WAR.
- **Android stays as it is.** The automatic build is free to keep. No touch controls, no phone testing.
- **No story campaign, no third gang, no new vehicle.**

## 5. Art pipeline

### Tools
- **Blockbench** (free). Made for exactly this kind of blocky low-poly model with pixel textures.
  You paint on the model directly, it has simple animation, and a beginner can make a prop on day one.
  This is the main tool.
- **Aseprite** (about $20). Pixel art for textures, icons and signs. Optional but worth it.
- **Blender** (free). Skip it for now. Learn it later only if Blockbench holds you back.
- **Asset packs** (free to about $30). PS1-style prop packs from itch.io and Kenney's free packs for
  street clutter. Buy props, make the characters yourself, so the stars of the game are yours.
- **AI model generators.** Not recommended. Their output is smooth and dense, the opposite of this
  style, and it is hard to animate.

### How a model gets into the game
1. You build and paint it in Blockbench.
2. **File > Export > Export glTF Model**, saved as a `.glb` file into a new `models` folder in the project.
3. You tell Claude it is there. Claude wires it into the scene, the shader and the colour system.

### Style rules, so everything matches
- Low triangle counts: props under 150, characters under 600.
- Small textures: 64x64, or 128x128 for characters. No smoothing or filtering.
- Chunky shapes over fine detail. If it reads at 240 pixels tall, it is right.

### What to replace first
1. **The cat.** On screen all the time and in every clip. Claude keeps the colour and accessory
   system working with the new model.
2. **The VAC-COP.** The face of the enemy and the co-star of every clip.
3. **Street props.** Bins, cones, vending machines, signs. These are also the things you knock over.
4. **The car.**
5. **NPCs.** Rebuilt from the new cat model plus outfits, so one good cat fixes fifteen characters.
6. **Weapons.** Last. They are small on screen and already read well.

## 6. Getting attention

- **The clip test.** Every feature should be judged by "would this make a 10-second clip?"
  Today's best clips: a Frozen Tuna launching a car, a bot vacuuming loot out of a cat, the breach.
- **One clip a week.** Record a short clip of whatever was built that week and post it on
  TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reddit (r/godot, r/IndieGaming). This habit matters more than any
  single trailer. Cats plus robot vacuums is a strong thumbnail.
- **itch.io page now.** Free, takes an evening, gives strangers a place to find and download the game.
- **Steam page in phase 2.** A one-time $100 fee. Free games do well there, Steam Deck players can
  find it, and Steam's friend invites replace router codes.
- **Store page needs:** a 30 to 45 second trailer that opens on the joke (the trailer tools already
  exist), five screenshots with the new models, cover art, and the pitch line.
- **Streamers come last.** Games like this spread when groups of streamers play together. That only
  works once joining is one click and there is voice chat.
- **Grow the Discord from playtests.** Every stranger who plays gets an invite link on the Raid Report.

## 7. Roadmap

"You" means things only you can do. "Claude" means things I build while you test.

### Phase 0: next 2 weeks. "Find out where the fun is"
**Goal:** learn from real play, and land two cheap wins.

- **You:** run one session with 3 friends. Stay quiet and record it. Write down 5 moments they
  laughed and 5 moments they were lost.
- **You:** install Blockbench and follow a beginner tutorial. Make one bin and one traffic cone.
- **You:** post the waiting patch notes to Discord.
- **Claude:** Raid Report screen.
- **Claude:** OVERCLEAN announcer (lines and a robot voice).
- **Claude:** co-op as the default mode, gangs moved to a TURF WAR option.

**Done when:** the playtest notes exist, a raid ends with a report, and your bin is standing on a street in the game.
**Not doing:** anything new to fight or shoot with.

### Phase 1: to mid January 2027. "A cat worth looking at, a raid worth retelling"
**Goal:** the game looks like a style and feels like being a cat.

- **You:** model the cat, then the VAC-COP. Expect several tries at the cat.
- **You:** build or buy a set of about 10 street props.
- **You:** set up the itch.io page and start the weekly clip.
- **Claude:** mess-making (knock-over props, scratching, bots that stop to clean).
- **Claude:** buyable cosmetics and boss-only rare drops.
- **Claude:** spectating after a knockout.
- **Claude:** hideout upgrades, first three.
- **Claude:** three or four district twists.
- **Claude:** two or three more music tracks using the existing music tools.

**Done when:** a friend who has not played for a month says it looks like a different game, and
your group chooses to play a second evening in a week without being asked.
**Not doing:** PvP features, new maps, voice chat, Steam.

### Phase 2: to May 2027. "Out in public"
**Goal:** strangers can find it, join each other in one click, and want to share it.

- **You:** pay for and set up the Steam page. Make the cover art and screenshots.
- **You:** car and NPC models.
- **You:** run a playtest with 10 or more strangers from the Discord or itch.io.
- **Claude:** Steam networking and friend invites.
- **Claude:** one place worth robbing (inside Meow-Mart), then a second.
- **Claude:** in-game voice chat.
- **Claude:** a new trailer with the trailer tools.
- **Claude:** more hideout upgrades and twists, based on what playtests ask for.

**Done when:** the Steam page is live with a trailer, and 10 people you have never met have
finished a raid together.
**Not doing:** campaign, third gang, touch controls, paid content.

### After that
Launch free on Steam, then decide from player numbers and feedback: more places to rob, more
cosmetics, or bringing TURF WAR up to the level of co-op.

3. **Steam in phase 2?** It costs $100 and is the main route to strangers.
